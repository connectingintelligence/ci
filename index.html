> building cultures that feel alive

What thoughts are thinkable from a coherent state that are unthinkable from a fragmented one?

We support teams at the AI frontier in creating cultures that feel safe and alive. Even under intense pressure, we want these teams to think clearly, disagree honestly, operate playfully and feel supported by each other.

We run offsites, workshops and team experiences that give people a taste of how fresh and playful working together can feel. We then look at the organisational level and help leaders integrate this into the way the team operates.

what we work from

  1. With trust, information flows differently.

    Trust changes what can be said, heard and felt in a team. With more trust, our attention and curiosity shift. We listen more deeply, and differing opinions become workable instead of threatening.

    Without trust, information gets noisy and fragmented. It becomes hard to tell the signal from noise, and everything gets slower and more expensive. In this way, trust is the foundational infrastructure of effective teams.

  2. Create containers that allow playful and safe experimentation.

    Experimenting is often the best path to something new. When we design new rules, rituals or structures for a team, we want to try things without harming the social environment they live in.

    As a team grows its capacity to run safe-to-fail experiments within its culture, what new possibilities emerge in its technical work?

  3. Walking the walk.

    We believe our bodies notice when something is off: a decision that doesn't sit right, a behaviour that costs us a night's sleep.

    Ethics as abstract principles can matter a lot. But what does it take to actually live them, day to day, even in the most challenging situations? Integrity lives in our bodies, our hearts and our minds, not only as ideas.

  4. Not bouncing back, but growing through.

    Resilience is often described as bouncing back: going through something hard and returning to how things were. We see it differently: it’s the ability to stay in relationship with a challenging situation, and to turn what happened into learning.

    When we only bounce back, we tend to repeat the same patterns. When we digest what happened, we get wiser.

    This is true for teams and organisations too. Collective resilience is a group's ability to stay in contact with what is hard, to see it together, and to turn it into shared learning, instead of moving on and repeating it.
